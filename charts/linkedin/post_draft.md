# LinkedIn post draft — country recipients and the mix behind them

Attach L01_top_recipients.png and L02_sector_composition.png, in that order.

---

Ten development finance institutions committed USD 585 billion between 2015
and 2024. I have been building a tracker that harmonises what all ten
disclose, and the country league table springs few surprises: Türkiye, India
and Egypt lead, and the top fifteen countries absorb a little over half of
every dollar that carries a named country.

The second chart changed how I read the first.

Break those fifteen countries into sectors and the aggregate pattern holds.
Banks and infrastructure take roughly two-thirds everywhere. Across all
country-specific lending the two account for 69% of committed value, and
across the top fifteen the median comes to 70%.

Then the pattern stops holding. The split between those two sectors moves
further than almost anything else in the data. Banking runs from 23% of
Morocco's book to 66% of Greece's. Infrastructure runs from 12% of Nigeria's
to 42% of Serbia's.

So the headline stays stable and tells you almost nothing. "Two-thirds goes
to financial institutions and infrastructure" describes the system and
describes no market inside it.

I tested the obvious explanation, that the mix follows whichever institution
happens to lend there. It explains some of the spread and not most.
Predicting each country's banking share from its lender composition alone
compresses the range to 26-44%, against an actual 23-66%, at a correlation of
0.37.

The rest belongs to the market. What a country absorbs reflects what reaches
bankability there, which is a different question from what it needs and a
different question from who is lending.

Nigeria makes the point. AfDB supplies 41% of Nigeria's development finance,
and AfDB puts 40% of its own global book into infrastructure. Nigeria still
takes 12% infrastructure, the lowest of the fifteen and the lowest by some
margin. That gap measures bankable pipeline rather than lender appetite.

Which is the question I would put to anyone building a market entry case:
when development capital arrives somewhere and settles almost entirely on
bank balance sheets, what does that tell you about the banks, and what does
it tell you about everything else that never reached financial close?

Method: ten institutions, 2015-2024, own-account only, country-specific deals
only, so every figure is a floor. Sector labels harmonised from ten separate
taxonomies. Full notes sit on both charts.

This piece reflects my own analysis. It does not constitute investment,
legal, or tax advice.

## Two things deliberately left out of the copy

**EBRD's Trade Facilitation Programme.** USD 17.9bn over 77 operations, all
inside Financial Institutions, carrying 22 points of Greece's banking share.
Excluding it the banking range still runs 14% to 49%, so the spread survives.
It sits in the chart note rather than the post because the post never leans
on Greece alone.

**AfDB amounts are a ceiling.** Its disclosure separates the government
contribution from the rest but does not separate AfDB from other
co-financiers. Stated on the charts.
